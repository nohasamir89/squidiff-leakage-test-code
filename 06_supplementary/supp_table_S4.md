Supplementary Table S4 | What the population distance of the original model is made of, on Tahoe-100M (Results 1; Fig. 1c). For each of the 2,899 held-out conditions of split 1, the cells generated by the original model (seed 0; at most 200 cells) were compared with the measured treated cells of the same condition (at most 200). a, Cells: mean over conditions, with the median in parentheses. Spread is the mean Euclidean distance between two cells of the same population; total expression is the sum of the 200 log-normalised expression values of a cell; zero entries are exact zeros (no generated value was below zero). The last column is the ratio of the two means, as printed in Fig. 1c. b, Distances and mean profiles. The population distance is the energy distance: twice the mean distance between a generated and a measured cell, minus the two spreads. The mean profile of a population is its mean over cells. Its correlation with the measured mean profile is the Pearson correlation across the 200 genes, and the slope is that of the least-squares line of the generated on the measured mean profile (1 means the same scale). Distances between means are Euclidean; the distance from the measured treated mean to the mean of the matched vehicle cells is the size of the measured response. The analysis was exploratory.
